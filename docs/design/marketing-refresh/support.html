Design notes

For the implementing agent. Each note carries the claim, why, and the file, test, or env var it binds to. Copy shown here is indicative: every visible string ships through the support.* marketing keys.

1
One header bar, not two. The current page renders its own styles.header row — back arrow, small logo, "Support" title — directly under the global MarketingHeader, so every visit opens with two stacked bars and a duplicated logo. Delete that block, the BackButton, and support.headerTitle's use in the page body; the global nav already tells you where you are. The page then starts at the hero, on the page ground. Binds: support-content.tsx:27-36 (header JSX), support.module.css .header / .headerTitle, site-chrome.tsx (the real header). Open: support.headerTitle is not in SUPPORT_COPY_KEYS, so dropping the key is safe for the disclosure test — confirm nothing else reads it before deleting it from four catalogs.
2
Exactly one h1, and it is the hero. "Keep Boardsesh running" is the only h1 on the page; every section below is an h2 and every rail an h3. The current code already fakes this with variant="h2" component="h1"; keep the semantics and let the type scale do the size. Nothing else on the page may be promoted to h1, state demos included. Binds: packages/web/app/support/__tests__/support-content.test.tsx:89-95 (asserts one h1, text equal to support.hero.title).
3
The ask is in the hero, as a real button. Today you read the whole hero, two prose paragraphs, and a section heading before you reach a bare MUI text link. Move the filled violet Sponsor on GitHub button into the hero, above the fold, and let the rails repeat it further down for the reader who scrolled. Active verb, no arrow glyph baked into the label — the button shape already says it is an action. Binds: GITHUB_SPONSORS_URL (support-content.tsx:17), support.sponsors.cta; --primary-fill from theme-config.ts, never a hardcoded hex.
4
The free-forever promise moves up, as a pull-quote. "Every part of Boardsesh stays open to everyone, sponsor or not" currently sits at the tail of support.honesty.p2, below the fold, in the same grey as the rest of the small print. It is the single line that makes the ask honest, so it belongs beside the ask. A left violet rule and a size bump give it weight without turning it into a card. The small print keeps the sentence too — the disclosure test reads the catalog, not the layout. Binds: support.honesty.p2; a new key (e.g. support.promise) must be added to all four locales — catalog-completeness.test.ts enforces parity.
5
Prose at .prose measure, on the page ground. The whole page is currently one MuiCard on a pale background, so a hero, three sections of prose, and two rails all share one flat plane and nothing reads as more important than anything else. Cards are for things you can act on. The "why" copy is reading, so it gets a measure (about 68ch) and no container at all. Binds: support-content.tsx:39-44 (the wrapping MuiCard/CardContent to remove), support.why.p1 / support.why.p2.
6
Heading is count-neutral: "Ways to chip in". The shipped heading says "Two ways to chip in" above one rail, because the second is gated on an env var that is unset in production. A heading must never promise a row it cannot render. Rewrite support.rails.title in all four locales; do not branch the string on stripeDonateUrl, which would make the copy untestable and the translations conditional. Binds: support.rails.title (currently "Two ways to chip in"), resolveStripeDonateUrl() (packages/web/app/support/stripe-donate-url.ts:14).
7
Rails are the only cards, and they get real buttons. .card.card-raised on --surface-2 so they lift off the ground rather than disappearing into a parent card, as they do today. GitHub Sponsors is the filled button, one-time is outlined: one primary action per section. The card CTAs are <a class="btn"> with a 44px min-height, not MuiLink text. Binds: support.module.css .rail / .railCta (both replaced), support.sponsors.*, support.oneTime.*. In prod these are MUI Buttons with theme tokens, never the style prop.
8
The grid degrades honestly to one rail. grid-template-columns: repeat(auto-fit, minmax(260px, 1fr)) replaces the current fixed repeat(2, minmax(0, 1fr)), which leaves a 50% hole whenever the Stripe rail is hidden — the production state today. With auto-fit one rail fills the row and the section still looks finished. State B above is what boardsesh.com renders right now; both states are drawn so the reviewer can check the degrade rather than imagine it. Binds: STRIPE_DONATE_URL (stripe-donate-url.ts:14, server-read, never NEXT_PUBLIC_); support-content.tsx:85-100 (the conditional rail, data-testid="support-one-time-rail"). Open: the Stripe Payment Link does not exist yet. Until someone creates it and sets the var on the Railway web service, State B is the only state that ships — so State B has to be the one that is reviewed.
9
"Other ways to help" is the page's second column of substance. With one donation rail the page had almost nothing in it. Four contribution routes — report a bug, translate a screen, send a patch, join the Discord — give the page weight that does not depend on an env var, and they are the routes that actually move the app. Wording must promise nothing in return: "report a bug" and "translate a screen" are safe, anything shaped like a thank-you gift is not. Binds: new support.help.* keys in four locales; donation-disclosure.test.ts bans unlock|early access|priority support|perk|reward|exclusive across SUPPORT_COPY_KEYS — add the new keys to that list so they are policed too.
10
Crawlable /about and /docs survive the redesign. The thanks block keeps the /about link and the "send a patch" card carries /docs. Both must be real LocaleLink anchors with descriptive text, not buttons or click handlers, or the page loses its internal-link floor and the test goes red. Binds: support-content.test.tsx:99-104 (hrefs contain /about and /docs); support.aboutLink, support.docsLink; LocaleLink from @/app/components/i18n/locale-link.
11
Small print looks like small print, and keeps its literal phrase. 13px, --secondary-label, one hairline above it, no card and no icon. What it may not do is soften: the string "not tax-deductible" is pinned per locale and asserted on the rendered text, so it survives any rewrite. Shrinking it visually is fine; paraphrasing it is not. Binds: support.honesty.p1; donation-disclosure.test.ts NOT_TAX_DEDUCTIBLE (en-US /not tax-deductible/i, plus es/fr/de); support-content.test.tsx:75-79.
12
"Buys nothing extra" stays, and no figures ever appear. The second small-print line keeps the buys-nothing promise verbatim. Separately: no hosting cost, no climber count, no revenue number anywhere on this page. Naming a figure invites "so you are nearly there?" and dates the copy the day the bill changes, which is why the ask is qualitative in every state. Binds: support.honesty.p2; donation-disclosure.test.ts perks regex. No binding to any metric — deliberately, there is none to bind.
13
Imagery is optional and must be a real photo. The page reads fine with none: the brand mark plus the hero glow carry it. If a photo is added later it goes below the rails, never above them, and it is a real session shot — AI-generated images are banned in this repo. The glow itself is a CSS radial-gradient, not an asset, so it costs nothing to ship. Binds: repo rule "No AI-generated images" (CLAUDE.md); hero glow is pure CSS on --primary-fill at low alpha. Open: is there a usable photo at all? If not, ship without the slot.
14
Reading order, top to bottom. Ask, promise, reason, rails, other help, thanks, small print, footer. The shipped order buries the ask under two paragraphs and puts the promise last. Anyone who arrives already convinced can act in the first screen; anyone who wants the reasoning finds it immediately after, and the legal text sits where legal text belongs. Binds: section order in support-content.tsx:38-145; i18n key order in marketing.json should follow the new order so translators read it the way users do.
