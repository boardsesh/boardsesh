# Account, activity, session and home-board privacy

Implementation issue: [#6242](https://github.com/boardsesh/boardsesh/issues/6242), under the Boardz follow-up epic [#6238](https://github.com/boardsesh/boardsesh/issues/6238). Privacy applies independently of any display preference (see [Make it configurable](./contributing-to-screens.md#make-it-configurable)).

## What a climber chooses

| Setting | Choices | Default and effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Account | Public, Private | New accounts start Public; Private is opt-in. Existing accounts keep their setting until they choose. Private activity is visible to its author and accepted followers. |
| Social item | Public, Followers, Only me | Private accounts default to Followers. A public exception needs confirmation; a later switch to Private invalidates earlier exceptions. |
| Session | Public, Anyone with the link (Unlisted), Followers, Invite-only | Public accounts default to Public; private accounts to Followers, for explicit and inferred sessions alike. Followers means accepted followers of the host. An Invite-only link requests access. The legacy Private switch (`is_public = false`) means Unlisted: the session leaves the live listings, nearby discovery and kiosk previews, and its invite link still joins. |
| Home board | Public, Unlisted, Followers, Invite-only | New home boards start Public with their exact location hidden. Unlisted means not listed, not secret: anyone with the link can open the board, see its presence and live queue preview, and start or join a session on it. The link is the board's uuid or its `/b/<slug>` path. A slug is built from the board's name, so it can be guessed, and a slug freed by a deleted board can be taken by a later board with the same name. A numeric board id is never the link, because ids are sequential: a presence read keyed on an id opens an Unlisted board only when the caller also sends its uuid. An Unlisted board stays out of discovery: nearby search, the gym directory, gym kiosks, the board picker rails and sitemaps. Invite-only boards return not-found to anyone without a grant, and a legacy private board to anyone but its owner. A Followers or Invite-only board also has a share link (`/b/<slug>?invite=<token>`, below): confirming Join while signed in makes the climber an approved member at once. Opening a board does not show its place; that follows its location audience. Owners may additionally inherit follower access. |
| Board location | Only me, Invited people, Everyone with board access | New home boards hide location. The owner's choice always governs the board's own coordinates and typed location name, at a gym or not: a gym never changes it. A viewer the board's own location is hidden from sees, instead, the location of a public, live gym the board is in: the gym's name and coordinates, which the gym already lists to everyone, and the gym link. With a private, deleted or no gym they see nothing. Known and accepted: a board created from a GPS fix with no gym picked is attached by the server to the one synced gym within about 150 m, even with its location hidden, and then shows that gym's place to people who can open the board. That reveals the board is near that gym, though never its own coordinates. Before this, such a board already appeared on the gym's page when it was public and listed. `projectBoardLocation` is that one rule; the board reads (`enrichBoard`, `enrichBoards`), the `UserBoard` location field resolvers (which re-read the gym from its row, never from the object a producer filled), the anonymous serial lookup, serial candidates and live-session gym names all use it, and a distance measured from the board's own coordinates goes only where those may. The app's location switch and buttons stay editable at a gym, with "At a public gym, people see the gym's location instead." under them. When the board's gym is a public gym the owner runs themselves (often a home wall that minted a public gym under the old rules), the board's edit screen and Manage access warn: "Your gym {{name}} is public, so its location is visible to everyone. If it's your home, make the gym private.", with a button to the gym's settings. Synced and other people's gyms never warn. Seeing a board does not necessarily reveal its address, coordinates, gym or distance. Followers is not offered: on a public account follows are approved automatically, so it would not restrict anything. A `followers` location sent by an older client is stored as Invited people; the value stays valid in the database so older rows keep working. |
| Stay anonymous on boards I don't own | On, Off | Off. On a board the climber doesn't own (a gym wall, a friend's home board, someone else's spray wall), its board-facing surfaces show them as the anonymous climber. A plain preference, available whether or not `BOARDSESH_PRIVACY_ENABLED` is set. See [Anonymous on boards they don't own](#anonymous-on-boards-they-dont-own). |

Account approval uses the existing `user_follows` relation for accepted followers and a separate pending request table. Following a private climber creates a request; it does not approve itself. The request reaches the private climber as a `follow_request` notification with Approve and Decline in the inbox. The row names the requester and shows only while the request is pending; approving or declining deletes it, and a cancelled request drops out of the inbox. A requester/recipient pair holds at most one row: a request inside the day-long dedup window shows the existing row again, and a later one replaces it. A decline leaves no row to hold that window, so asking again straight after a decline is a fresh unread row. Every write to a pair's row (the notification worker, the inline fallback, approve, decline, and a follow of a public account) takes the same per-pair advisory lock first, before any row lock. Approving does not also send the owner a "started following you" notification. Approve and Decline work with the controls switched off, because a rollback leaves installed clients' `followUser` creating requests and the owner must still be able to answer them. The legacy `followUser` mutation returns `false` when it only left a request, so an installed client that reads the result does not show the requester as following. Existing followers keep access when the account becomes private, and the climber can remove them. Approvals and removal use the target account's authority.

Item controls accompany tick, comment, playlist and beta publication. Existing items have an editable audience indicator. Session posts use the session audience.

Activity follows its author. A tick is readable when its author's account privacy and the tick's own audience allow it; the session it was logged in and the board it was logged on never hide it. Board privacy hides the board's identity on the item instead: its name, location, gym, coordinates and distance are blank for a viewer who cannot see the board, and the row stays. An unlisted board is named only to people who already hold it, since a log is not a link. On logs, beta and feeds, holding it means owning it, an approved grant, or follower inheritance (`boardIdentityVisibleCondition`). The live-sessions rail and board sheet also count an unlisted board the viewer follows or has selected, because they reached it through its link. Their board projections apply the current audience and grants before returning a name or UUID, so a revoked board grant hides its identity while the session remains readable. A session on a named board (`/b/<slug>`) returns its board path as geometry (`/<type>/<layout>/<size>/<sets>/<angle>`) to a viewer who cannot see the board, so the invite link still joins without naming it. For a live home board, a caller whose own create/join input names it and the actual actor changing its path keep that presented name while the board's link still opens it for them. Reconnect snapshots use current board standing; being the session's creator does not prove who supplied its mutable current path. That covers the session payload, roster snapshots, `SessionBoardPathChanged` and nearby discovery. A soft-deleted board keeps its row, so its slug still resolves to geometry; only its owner and anyone holding an approved grant on it keep the named path. A slug no board row carries any more (renamed since, or its account deleted) comes back as the empty path. A public `userTicks` logbook returns a tick's `sessionId` only to the tick's climber, since a session id is its invite link. In a multi-user session each participant's ticks follow that participant's account; the session's name, notes and roster follow the session's own audience. A missing, unresolvable or soft-deleted parent board never hides the item, and never withholds a session's place. Its identity follows the same rule as any board the viewer cannot see: a deleted board is no longer named (its fields stay blank, and the row stays), except in its climber's own logbook. A comment's audience still cannot widen its thread, and a tick still cannot reveal a climb whose own audience has narrowed. Spray walls keep their separate wall rule. Joining a session or using Bluetooth does not approve access to another climber's activity. Profile name/avatar remain discoverable so someone can request a follow; activity, linked board identities and personal counts are withheld from unauthorized profile visitors.

Tick board correction reads never treat a stored numeric board attribution as an Unlisted link. Historical ticks can have been associated through a numeric ID or a session, so the current-board picker uses current owner, grant or follower access. An edit that explicitly supplies a verified board UUID may name that Unlisted board in its immediate response; an ordinary later edit does not retain that link provenance. A supplied UUID of a board that was merged away is a link to the survivor only when one owner holds both boards: another owner's Unlisted survivor gets the no-link reading, so a tick edit refuses it (`TICK_BOARD_UNAVAILABLE`) and a new tick saves without that board.

A climb has no audience. Published (`is_draft IS NOT TRUE`) and listed (`is_listed IS TRUE`), it is public to everyone, signed in or not, whoever set it: no account privacy, item audience, follow relation or deleted-account record is consulted for it anywhere, from search, counts and climb pages to stats, similar climbs, duplicates, feeds, playlists, proposals, climb comment and vote threads, beta links, ticks, sitemaps, OG cards, setter pages and kiosk, queue and Live Activity projections. A tick is never hidden because of who set its climb. A draft is returned to its owner alone (see [Drafts](#drafts)). An unlisted climb is left out of lists, search, the ascent, activity and comment feeds, notification inboxes and proposals (the moderation feed and the climb's own proposal list), for everyone but its owner, but a direct link still resolves it, and its own page shows its logs and comment thread. The board sync still carries it, marked unlisted, and the app's lists hide it. A playlist or a liked list shows an unlisted climb it holds to whoever may open the list, and a draft to the draft's owner alone. A session card and the session summary name every climb sent in the session, a draft, someone else's unlisted climb and a climb the community hid included: sending a climb is the climber's activity, and a draft sent to a session is fine (owner decision, 2026-10-13). A board's hardest send never features an unlisted climb to anyone but its owner, or a climb the community hid to anyone. Spray-wall visibility is a separate rule about the wall and still applies to its climbs. Who set a published climb is public with it: a shared climb database is public by definition, and a private account does not change that. The climb's setter name travels with it everywhere, search included. Its author's Boardsesh account id (`user_id`) travels with it in the board sync behind an offline download, whatever the account's privacy, so anyone who knows an account's id can list the published climbs it set. The public snapshot dataset carries no climb a Boardsesh account set (see below). A few online surfaces still show the account's display name, avatar and account id only to a viewer who may see its activity, and show everyone else the setter name. They are `linkedUser*` and the setter hero on `setterProfile`; `userClimbs`, which returns nothing for them; the GraphQL `Climb.userId` field, resolved per viewer after the shared search cache (the www climb page never sends it); and the board's new-climb feed, its live push and the crew feed card. That is how those surfaces present the climb, not a promise that the link from a climb to its account is hidden. A notification or activity-feed row about a climb names any actor on it, its author included, only to a viewer who may see that account's activity. A stranger watching a layout still gets the new-climb notification about a private account's climb, under its setter name with no account id or avatar, the way the board's new-climb feed shows it. Following a setter name needs no approval, because the setter page is public. What a private account keeps private is its activity: ticks, sessions, comments and likes. Joining a session or using Bluetooth does not approve access to another climber's activity. Profile name/avatar remain discoverable so someone can request a follow; activity, linked board identities and personal counts are withheld from unauthorized profile visitors.

Ticks continue contributing to numeric climb and board aggregates. Board history retains events with private attribution removed. Projections remove real account IDs, names, avatars, identifying comments and the details of drafts and of climbs on walls the viewer cannot see. Where realtime protocols require participant IDs, the backend uses session-scoped opaque identifiers. An aggregate total is never a list of private climbers with stable pseudonyms.

### Anonymous on boards they don't own

Issue [#4189](https://github.com/boardsesh/boardsesh/issues/4189) asked to keep sends off gym wall feeds. The board keeps its full history instead, and the climber is shown as anonymous. The preference is `user_profiles.anonymous_on_foreign_boards`, set by `setAnonymousOnForeignBoards` (not flag-gated) and read back on `privacySettings`. Settings → Privacy is always listed, so the switch is reachable whether or not `BOARDSESH_PRIVACY_ENABLED` is set; without the flag the screen shows the switch and a one-line note in place of the account, session-default and follow-request controls. Saving publishes no `privacyChanged`: board-facing reads apply the preference at query time, and live streams re-project on their next event. Preference saves have their own five-per-minute rate limit across connections, separate from account privacy and session operations. Account and anonymity controls wait for either save to finish. Saved state comes from a fresh read; when that read fails, the screen keeps the value the server acknowledged. Each write keeps the account and credential generation captured when it was requested, so late replies or queued requests cannot affect a replacement account.

Nothing changes on the write path. Ticks and `board_climb_events` rows are stored as before, so board stats, distinct-climber counts and leaderboard totals keep counting the climber. The projection happens at read time, which makes it retroactive (old history rows are covered) and reversible (switching it off names the climber again). A screen that already drew a name keeps it until its next read or live event; a signed-out kiosk TV keeps it until it reloads.

One predicate decides it: `anonymousOnBoardCondition` in `packages/db/src/queries/privacy.ts`. It holds when the author switched the preference on, they don't own the board the row is about, and the viewer is not the author. It fails closed: a board that no longer exists, a board the app created only to climb on someone else's wall (`is_owned = false`), or no known board at all, is not theirs. In a live session, owning the session's board is not enough on its own, because any member can move a session onto someone's public board: the owner is named there only after their own tick or durable lit climb on that board in that session. Stored sessions do not record who chose their board, so a creator who starts a session on their own board stays unnamed to others until that first action. Known gap, not a regression against main (which names everyone): on a personal LED wall reached by its config path, a joiner whose app has selected their own board of the same model has their sends and lit climbs filed under that board, so they count as acting on their own board, and another member moving the session onto that board or logging a send there can then name them. A creator who starts a session from their own same-model board while standing at someone else's wall is not covered either; without a trusted wall identity the server cannot tell that from being at home. A wall feed, its stats, leaderboard, Bluetooth holder, trending sends and session cards are about their board.

Inside an identified restricted board, its owner and explicit approved board members may see climbers’ names without following their private accounts. This bypasses the extra anonymous-board preference for that projection. The effective audience must be Only me, Followers or Invite only, and the viewer must still pass current board access; revocation applies immediately. Public and Unlisted boards, inherited followers without an approved grant, deleted boards and unidentified config paths never grant this exception. The named climber must also have logged a tick or lit a climb on that exact board; live sessions require their own action in that same session. Another member rebinding a public session, or unilaterally inviting the climber, is not evidence. Since stored sessions do not identify who last chose their board path, even the creator stays unnamed to non-followers until their own tick or durable lit-climb event supplies that evidence. Durable wall events require the existing 60-second presence gate and another report after it, so a first lit climb alone may not release the name. Profile, logbook and global feed policy stays unchanged; board membership does not reveal private tick notes or clips. Recent-sender GraphQL fields retain their server-resolved board context and recheck current access in one batch; an unrelated object keeps account-only projection. Public ticks still require consent at the current account revision.

A live session is about the one board it is on, the same board for its Climbing now card and for every surface of the session the card opens. `resolveSessionBoards` (`services/session-board.ts`) picks it, the session's own word first:

1. its primary board (`board_sessions.board_id`);
2. the board its path names (`/b/<slug>`, or a legacy `/spray/<layout>`);
3. the board a climb lit through Boardsesh bound it to (`session:{id}:board`, written by `reportBoardClimb`);
4. the board of its newest tick, skipping a tick on a board its author owns.

Boards attached to a session (`session_boards`) never decide it, so a session on the climber's own garage with a gym wall attached names them. A session nothing places is judged as not theirs, even when the climber owns or follows a board with the same config. Shared layout, size and hold sets do not identify the actual wall. That is also why step 4 skips a tick on its author's own board: with no wall to go on, `saveTick` files a config-path send under the author's own board of that config, and the app joining a config-path session selects (or creates) the joiner's own board of that config, so such a tick names a config the climber holds, not the wall they are at. So a session on a personal LED wall reaches that board's presence sheet and the followed-board list on Climbing now only after a climb is lit through Boardsesh (step 3); the followed-creator and followed-climber lists still show it. A Redis error while reading the binding stops the search there, so an outage fails closed instead of guessing from ticks. A Bluetooth serial does not place a session: the supplier reuses serials across walls.

When the session's wall changes, connected peers re-project what they already hold. Every committed path change publishes `SessionBoardPathChanged`; a move to another wall marks it `wallChanged`, and each peer's `sessionUpdates` then sends a projected `UserPresenceChanged` per member, the path and a `SessionRosterSnapshot`, while its `queueUpdates` sends a fresh `FullSync`. An angle change keeps the wall and sends only the path. A wall that moves under an unchanged path, when a lit climb binds a session with no primary board to another wall (`reportBoardClimb`) or a newer send moves it (`saveTick`), publishes the same event marked `pathUnchanged`: peers re-project and send no path event. `wallChanged` and `pathUnchanged` are server-only fields on the session channel; GraphQL never serializes them. Who may be named is decided by the read rules above, never by the refresh, so a stranger's move onto someone's board refreshes peers with the same anonymous rows.

Server projections attach session/board provenance to cloned roster, queue and hardest-send objects in WeakMaps. GraphQL fields recheck the projection's source even on HTTP or a connection joined to another session; retained objects recheck current grants after revocation. Private-board members may see an actor's default hardest-send identity, but explicit tick restrictions still apply. Notes and featured clips always retain the tick's ordinary author/item permission.

Surfaces fold the predicate into the identity lookup they already make, one query per page: `visibleActivityUserIds(userIds, viewer, onBoard)` for one board, `sessionsVisibleUserIds` for live sessions (Climbing now, nearby discovery), `sessionAnonymousUserIds` where identity follows the logs' own audience (session detail, the session summary), and `anonymousOnBoardUserIds` for the hardest send and the board feeds. The `ClimbQueueItem` and `SessionUser` field projections batch through `batchedSessionVisibleUserIds`, one board resolution and one query per projection; a producer whose caller has no session of its own (the ESP32 `navigateQueue`) projects the item against its session first. Every surface uses the same anonymous projection as a private account, so a page of history never turns into a list of stable pseudonyms:

- `boardHistory`, `boardHistoryPage`, `boardRecentHistory`, `boardRecentClimbs` and the `boardNowPlaying` stream (`redactBoardClimbs`, `redactBoardEvent`): the row stays with no sender id, name or avatar.
- `boardPresenceStats` hardest send (`redactBoardStats`) and `boardClimbRecentSenders`.
- `boardLeaderboard` (also the kiosk leaderboard rail): the entry keeps its rank and totals with no identity.
- The Bluetooth holder (`boardConnection`, `BoardConnectionChanged`) and the holder name in other participants' Live Activity pushes.
- A board's own feeds: `sessionGroupedFeed({ boardUuid })` (Home's gym mode) unnames the card's owner, participants, hardest send and featured beta; `trendingFeed({ boardUuid })` keeps the send without actor or note.
- A spray wall's climb logs (`climbLogs` for a spray climb): the wall is a board, so a log there keeps its place under a per-log opaque id, with no name, avatar or note. Catalogue climbs (Kilter and the rest) belong to no one board and are unchanged.
- The roster, queue `addedBy`/`tickedBy`, host and Live Activity holder of a live session, keeping the session-scoped opaque participant ids; nearby discovery's host.
- The same session's `sessionDetail` (the in-session leaderboard and tick list, the `SessionStatsUpdated` push, the web session page and its OG card) and `sessionSummary`: rows and counts stay, under the session's opaque participant id, without name, avatar, note or clips.
- Climbing now (`liveSessions`): the same, and following the climber does not change it; the preference beats the follow. A followed climber who stays anonymous on a session's board doesn't list that session as one they are in, lift it up the rail or count among its followed climbers; the session can still appear for its board, with the climber counted and unnamed.

The climber sees their own name everywhere. Their own boards, their logbook, profile and follower feeds (the crew feed, `followingClimbAscents`) are unaffected; the account Public/Private rule governs those. The unscoped Everyone feed is not board-facing either: it follows the author's account privacy. Following a climber never names them on a board they don't own. The board queue preview and the watch endpoint carry no climber identity to begin with. The preference is global for now; a per-gym choice can follow.

### Sharing a board

The owner of a Followers or Invite-only home board gets a share link from the app's Share sheet: `/b/<slug>?invite=<token>`. The token is 128 random bits, minted server-side (`board_share_links`, migration `0267_board_share_links.sql`), one active link per board. Opening the link only previews the board name. A signed-in climber must explicitly choose "Join {{board name}}?" before becoming an approved member: the redemption writes the same `resource_grants` row an owner's name invite writes, with `invited_by` set to the owner, so they read the board, its presence and its sessions on the next request, and they show as approved on the owner's access screen, where the owner can remove them. Anyone holding the link can add themselves, so that list is paged (100 at a time, pending requests first, then newest), and a search reads each result's grant from the server instead of from the loaded pages. Signed out, the app keeps the link through sign-in and then asks them to join; the web page names the board (its name only, never its place) and offers a sign-in that comes back to the same URL and the Join confirmation. The prompt explains that the owner can see their name in the member list. Web GET, browser prefetch and link previews never write a grant; only the explicit POST server action redeems the token. On mobile, cancelling the prompt, navigating away or changing accounts while it is open writes no grant. "Reset link" revokes the token and mints a new one; people who already joined keep their access. A revoked or unknown token answers not-found, and the link then opens like the plain `/b/<slug>`. Moving the board to Public, Unlisted or Only me retires the link, so it does not come back to life if the board is made shareable again. A climber the owner removed stays removed whatever link they hold; the owner can invite them again by name. Redemption takes the owner's `users` row before the board row, the same order as `updateResourcePrivacy`, and a failed statement that carried the token is rethrown with its SQLSTATE only, so a database outage cannot log it. Public and Unlisted boards share their plain link, an Only-me board has none, and spray walls keep their own `?wall=` link. The token stays out of logs, analytics and shared caches: www answers token-bearing URLs `no-store` with `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`, including sign-in callbacks, nested return URLs and locale redirects, the Cloudflare HTML rule bypasses it, PostHog stays off on a URL carrying it, Sentry events have it redacted, and every hop that redeems it redirects to the clean URL. HTTP and WebSocket parse, validation and coercion errors for the two token-bearing operations discard echoed token values and retained document/error details before responses and generic logs, keeping only a safe code, a retry delay and an HTTP status. Errors the two resolvers raise themselves carry a response path and never the token, so they pass unchanged: a rate limit keeps `retryAfterSeconds` for the app's single retry.

## Enforcement and writes

`packages/shared/privacy` defines renderer-independent policy. `packages/db/src/queries/privacy.ts` expresses the policy inside queries, before pagination and counts. Backend runtime helpers in `services/privacy.ts` authorize individual resources. Policy reads use the primary database: a lagging replica cannot restore a revoked grant.

Session authorization reads the session's own policy only. Its primary and attached boards, and the boards named by legacy named-board and spray paths, are looked up for one purpose: a session's coordinates and distance are withheld unless every live board it names lets the viewer see its location. A missing or soft-deleted board on those paths withholds nothing. A local PostgreSQL `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)` fixture with 2,500 boards and 25 sessions measured the primary/attached-board lookup at 0.7 ms, compared with 317 ms for scanning the board registry first; the old estimated cost also triggered JIT compilation. An account/content count over 10,000 climbs (1,000 authored) took 27 ms and used the policy, profile and follow indexes. These are fixture measurements, not production latency targets; profile feed and sync plans on representative data before increasing rollout.

Migration `0261_account_resource_privacy.sql` adds account privacy revisions and onboarding completion, content policies, resource policies/grants and follow requests. Onboarding completion is no longer read: the forced setup screen is retired (see the release sequence), and the column waits for a later migration to drop it. Legacy rows remain supported. New native and web accounts are created public whatever the controls flag says: the profile row carries only the user id, and the column defaults (`is_private = false`, no session default) make it public. Existing private choices are never reset when the flag is disabled, and a missing profile on an existing account remains public for compatibility.

A board created with none of the visibility fields, which is what an installed client may send, lands public with its location hidden and no `resource_privacy` row, so the legacy booleans keep their roundtrip. An explicit `isPublic: false` still makes it Only me. A board auto-created when a climber connects to a wall's serial gets the same defaults. A gym minted alongside a new board is public only when the board's audience is Public (not Unlisted) and its exact location is shown: a gym is listed with its name and coordinates, so a minted gym must never publish a hidden or link-only address. A gym minted from a typed location is not public yet, so the board keeps the location it asked for: a home wall's typed location must not end up on the map. An Unlisted board at a gym therefore still mints a private gym.

Migration `0269_privacy_public_defaults_backfill.sql` puts back the public defaults for rows created since #6254 merged (2026-10-08 21:27:51 UTC, assuming `created_at` holds UTC) that still look exactly like the flipped default: private with the location hidden and never edited (`updated_at = created_at`), with either no policy row or an untouched `only_me` policy whose location is also `only_me` (revision 0), no grants, not deleted, not spray walls (a spray wall is created private on purpose) and not owned by the system catalogue account. A private board with its location shown, or one switched private after creation, was a deliberate choice and stays private. So does every default-shaped board of an owner who chose privacy anywhere else: a deliberately private account, a board or session policy they edited, an Only me session or item, or a board they made private before the cutoff, when the default was public. The residual is accepted by the owner: on the #6254 bundle private was the preselected default, so a board left private on purpose by someone with no other privacy signal goes public; the dry run counts them. Accounts still private with the followers session default, privacy revision 0, setup never completed and never edited go public. Every stored `followers` location narrows to `members`. That block never touches `hide_location`. A board in a public gym keeps its hidden location too: viewers see the gym's place at read time. A `_bs_migration_guards` row makes it run once. The file carries a `SELECT count(*)` for each update, plus one for default-shaped boards edited since creation that it leaves for the owner to decide.

Turn `BOARDSESH_PRIVACY_ENABLED` on only after the OTA carrying this change has reached phones: the previous builder preselects Invite-only for a new board once the controls are on.

Comment references use a bounded recursive query: every intermediate comment must be readable, and a missing, cyclic or over-depth entity reference fails closed. A comment is its author's activity, read through its own audience and the audience of every reply ancestor above it, so a public reply never widens a private parent. Three exemptions sit on top (`commentPrivacyCondition`):

- The owner of the commented item reads every comment and like on it, whatever the commenter's account privacy: the tick's climber, the session's host, the climb's setter, the playlist's owner, a comment's author (for likes on it), and a live board's or gym's owner (`entityOwnerCondition`). That includes a comment whose own audience is Only me or Followers: the app's composer offers no audience, but an API caller can set one, and it never hides a comment from the owner of the item it is on. The spray wall rule and a climb's draft and listing rules still apply to owners. A proposal has no owner: it is moderation on someone else's climb, so its proposer reads other people's comments on it, and hears about its votes and outcome, by the same account rule as anyone else.
- An owner is notified only where Boardsesh already sends a notification. Comments and likes on your tick (`comment_on_tick`, `vote_on_tick`) and likes on your comment (`vote_on_comment`) arrive whatever the actor's account privacy. A reply to your comment (`comment_reply`) follows the replier's privacy, like any reply. Session hosts, climb setters, playlist owners and board or gym owners get no comment or like notifications, and `comment_on_climb` has no sender ([#6385](https://github.com/boardsesh/boardsesh/issues/6385)).
- Whoever replied in a thread reads every comment above their reply. The viewer's own replies are walked up to their roots once per statement (a hashed set, not a walk per row), so the parent they answered stays readable to them, and so do public replies beside theirs. A private reply beside theirs stays hidden, and so does everything under it. On a 200-comment thread this read takes 2.1 ms against 6.6 ms for a per-row walk. A read about one item (a thread, adding or editing a comment, a deleted-comment event) starts the walk from the viewer's comments on that item only, because a reply always shares its parent's item. Feeds and the inbox, which span items, walk from all of them. On a 600,000-comment fixture with production's planner settings, a viewer with 10,000 replies across the site paid 267 ms for the site-wide walk and 0.5 ms for the walk scoped to the thread.
- A reply ancestor that no longer exists is readable. Deleting an account cascades its comments; the replies under them stay readable instead of orphaned. A missing ancestor stops blocking the chain and nothing more: each surviving reply still follows its own author's account privacy and its own audience, exactly as it would under a live public ancestor.

Reply ancestry must still end at a root or a missing row within 32 ancestors. Cycles and longer chains fail closed for everyone, owners and authors included. The global comment feed disables PostgreSQL JIT only inside its read transaction. A small local fixture returned three comments in 105 ms rather than 11 seconds; `EXPLAIN ANALYZE` attributed 10.9 seconds to JIT compilation. Permissions and pagination still run in SQL, and the transaction leaves database defaults unchanged.

A climb input still accepts the deprecated `privacy` field, ignored, so installed builds are not rejected. One exception: a create or edit that asks for "Only me" or "Followers" (an un-updated app, or a publish queued offline before the update) saves the climb as a draft, owner-only, instead of silently publishing it to everyone. An edit never turns an already published climb back into a draft. Publication inputs for other content carry an audience and the account's privacy revision. Writes save content and its policy in the same transaction. A stale explicit Public choice fails with `PRIVACY_REVISION_CONFLICT`, so a saved draft or queued mutation cannot silently undo a subsequent privacy change. Omitting privacy on an old client's edit preserves existing policy. Old board/session visibility booleans cannot widen a newer resource policy, and stale location toggles cannot reveal an address; use the audience control to widen access. Resources created with only legacy booleans retain no policy override, so installed clients can still switch Public to Private and back. A modern audience/location choice or restrictive account session default establishes an override that legacy booleans cannot widen. A legacy playlist Public-to-Private transition also withdraws explicit public consent while retaining invited collaborators. Privacy-bearing queued publications still commit during a controls rollback, with revision checks unchanged. Mutations verify ownership under row locks; approval cannot race a resource ownership change.

Account deletion materializes restrictions before clearing author references. Content and resource policies survive as ownerless records, preventing personal beta and restricted sessions from becoming public after deletion. A deleted author's published climbs stay public with no author. Their drafts are deleted with the account. An unlisted climb loses its owner, so it drops out of every list for everyone and still opens by its link. No climb policy is written. Every climb the account authored keeps an internal `is_boardsesh_authored` marker: snapshot exports exclude those climbs, and the first-ascent projection resolves current tick permission instead of trusting a stored name that may be the deleted account's. Imported manufacturer data is untouched. The transaction preserves numeric climb aggregates and retained board/history references, then broadcasts cache invalidation after commit.

Migration `0276_drop_climb_content_privacy.sql` removes stored climb policies, including deleted-account tombstones. First it re-stamps the sync cursor of every published climb a private account set, and of every climb with a policy row, with their stats and grades rows, so a phone that already pulled past them receives them. The owner approved this production data deletion on 2026-10-12, to run on the deploy after the change merges; the read-only standby count was zero that day at 01:44:35 UTC (#6394). `content_privacy_type_check` still allows `climb` for one release, because migrations run before the backend deploys and a rollback keeps the schema, so the previous backend may still write a climb policy in that window; nothing reads one. The contract step (#6376) deletes any such row and narrows the CHECK.

`resource_privacy` overrides legacy visibility fields. Board/session access and location are checked separately. Revocation overrides inherited follower access. Switching an Unlisted board to Invite-only withdraws direct-link access. Every gate reads the effective audience (the policy row first, the legacy booleans second), so a board saved Unlisted with the current controls (`is_public = false`) opens exactly like a legacy one. Each read names the key it was given. A board's uuid and a home board's `/b/<slug>` path are its link. A numeric board id, a serial, a listing, a log and a spray wall's slug are not, so they take the no-link reading, where an Unlisted board opens only for people who already hold it (owner, grant, follower inheritance). Reads keyed on a numeric id (presence, history, stats, the queue preview, a board's live sessions, the boards attached to a new session) accept the board's uuid alongside the id to unlock the link reading. A slug that reaches a board through a merge tombstone is that board's link only when one owner holds both boards. A legacy `board_sessions.is_public = false` without an override reads as Unlisted, and old clients' Private switch writes Unlisted; only an explicit audience asks for Invite-only. Listings (live sessions, nearby sessions, kiosk previews, and session cards in the session feed and Home) use the no-link rule, so an Unlisted session appears there only for its host and the people who joined it. The global comment feed and the activity feed are discovery too: a comment on an Unlisted session reaches them only for that host and roster (`listedSessionCondition`), since the comment carries the session id, which is its link. A comment on an Unlisted board, or a comment that references one, reaches them only for the people who already hold that board (its owner, an approved grant, or follower inheritance), since the global comment feed returns the board's UUID as the comment's `entityId`. The session's or board's own thread, votes and the notifications addressed to someone in the thread keep the link reading. A tick in a session the viewer cannot open as a card still follows its author: it lands on the climber's daily card for that day, without the session's name or roster. Readable logs outside the viewer's session cards stay on that daily card even when the same day also has an openable session; only logs already represented by a session card are excluded. The old client's "Show this session live" switch moves a session between Public and Unlisted in both directions. Inferred session splits copy privacy and grants; merges preserve the restrictive intersection, and that policy covers the session's name, notes and roster only. Moving ticks between sessions never rewrites their audience: reconciliation writes no `content_privacy` rows. Migration `0265_privacy_reconcile_tick_tombstones.sql` removes the Only-me rows earlier reconciliation stamped on ticks it moved into explicit sessions, and rewrites the session policies the legacy switch wrote as Invite-only after #6254 to Unlisted. Its precondition was confirmed by the owner on 2026-10-12: the flag (`BOARDSESH_PRIVACY_ENABLED`) was never enabled in production. Moving comments out of a restricted session still keeps them restricted.

## Drafts

A draft climb is returned to its owner and to nobody else. That covers the climb and everything read from it: its name, holds, setter, stats and grades rows, and whether it exists. The owner is the Boardsesh `user_id` on the climb. A draft with no owner is returned to no one.

In the online reads the draft rule is the whole climb rule for a read by reference. `climbDraftVisibilityCondition` in `packages/db/src/queries/privacy.ts` is that rule, and `climbReferenceReadableCondition` applies it to a stats, grades or neighbours row keyed by a climb reference. A list uses `publicClimbCondition` (published and listed) or, where the viewer's own drafts and unlisted climbs belong in it, `ownedOrPublicClimbCondition`. A query with no owner to serve filters `is_draft = false`. `is_draft` is nullable and NULL is not a draft, so the rule is written `IS NOT TRUE` / `IS TRUE`.

Where it holds:

- **Board sync and snapshot artifacts.** The board sync sends a draft to its owner alone; snapshot artifacts carry no draft (see below).
- **Reads by reference.** Climb detail, per-angle stats and stats history, Boardsesh grades, the grade outlier analysis, playlist climbs, liked-climbs playlists, the live similar-climbs target, the board's hardest send, and the public climb pages and REST routes on www (which have no viewer, so they serve no draft at all).
- **Lists.** Search, setter and profile pages, feeds, recommendations, similar climbs, popularity and sitemaps filter `is_draft = false`. The drafts list compares the row's `user_id` with the caller and nothing else, so it lists exactly the drafts that open for them. Session cards, the session summary and climb history are not reads of the climb: they name a draft sent in the session.

## Reads, live updates and downloaded copies

Feeds, profiles, search, comments, votes, notifications, playlists, beta links, session views and board history authorize their current viewer. Ticks follow their author; a feed scoped to one board (trending, the session feed's board filter, the materialized feed's board filter) first requires access to that board. Board history and the Bluetooth holder name a climber by their account privacy, whatever session the event belonged to, and never on a board they don't own once they choose to stay anonymous there. Comments are capped by their thread, except for the item's owner and the people who replied in it. A gym's thread reads exactly when the gym does. `gym` shows a private gym only to its owner, its admin and editor members, and community admins or leaders whose role is global or covers a board type live at the gym (`userCanEditGym`); its comments, likes, notifications and feed entries stay with the same people (`gymVisibleCondition`, the SQL form of that rule). A plain gym member does not see them. A public gym's thread reads for everyone, signed in or not. Climb proposals are moderation on a published climb: the reason, vote tally and comment thread read for everyone who can read the climb, and only a private proposer's or resolver's id, name and avatar are withheld. Each comment in a proposal thread still follows its author's privacy, for the proposer too, so the first reporter does not read a later private reporter's words or name unless they follow them. The same rule decides whether the proposer's inbox names a private moderator or voter. Voting on a proposal needs the same read access to its climb as the listings; anything else reads as not found. Public web rendering and OG previews use anonymous backend authorization. Sensitive responses avoid shared persistent caches; sitemaps reauthorize persisted candidates before returning their URLs. Private profiles and settings are not indexed.

Published climbs are public independently of their setter's account, and so is who set them. The account's activity keeps its own permissions. Privacy-filtered sitemap responses remain uncached so unlisting, moderation and wall changes can withdraw their URLs.

`socialEntityPrivacyCondition` and `tickPrivacyCondition` use a climb scope: `listed` for a feed, inbox or list; `by-reference` for one entity opened by its ID, including a climb's logs, comments and votes. Proposals always use `listed`. Entity discovery also applies the no-link session rule independently of the climb scope. Moderation `hide` remains a separate rule on every browse surface, including the live new-climb push.

The setter page and setter OG card read on the primary: a setter's climbs can span spray walls, and replica lag must not expose a wall that just became private.

The authenticated `privacyChanged` stream carries an opaque Boolean, including on reconnect. It discloses no changed account or resource IDs. The website listens: after a committed change it cancels requests, clears protected query snapshots and refetches. The app does not listen (owner decision, 2026-10-12). Every read it makes is authorized by the server at that moment, live streams recheck access on each event, and it never stores other people's feeds, sessions or profiles: those are fetched on each view. Live iterators recheck access even when no ordinary activity event arrives. Redis carries invalidation between backend instances. Retiring the stream on the backend is [#6406](https://github.com/boardsesh/boardsesh/issues/6406).

Every connected website tab, and every app bundle from before the app stopped listening, reacts to every event, so the backend publishes on these commits only: switching the account between public and private; `setContentAudience` and `updateResourcePrivacy`; approving a follow request; removing a follower who was following; approving, revoking or inviting resource access when the grant's status changes; deleting an account; editing a comment, tick or playlist with an explicit audience; editing a playlist, session or board whose stored visibility actually flips, or a board whose location visibility changes; a tick save, edit or delete whose inferred-session regrouping takes access away (a merge whose intersected policy is narrower than one of its sources, content restricted to its author, or a tick moved under a narrower explicit session); a live session moving onto a wall that some viewer cannot read without a link (not public, or public with a revoked viewer), decided inside the move's transaction under a share lock on the wall's row, so an edit to that wall lands before the decision or waits for the move. Edit screens that resend an unchanged `isPublic` with a rename publish nothing. These do not publish: following or unfollowing (users and setters), follow and access requests, cancelling or declining a request, the privacy onboarding and settings save unless the account switch flips (the default session audience only applies to sessions created later), renames, notes and description edits, saving a tick or editing one without an audience when regrouping narrows nothing (a merge of equal audiences, a split, a new session, and the backfill), and moving a live session onto a public wall or back onto one it already references. Every wall switch still re-derives the public kiosk previews, and live session streams recheck access on their next event. Deleting a board has never published, although it hides the board from everyone but its owner; that is a known gap. The app that made a follow change refreshes its own caches. A publish goes out as soon as its write commits, before the public kiosk previews are refreshed: that refresh does its own Redis and database reads and can fail, and a failure must not hold back the invalidation. Each publish writes one `[Privacy] privacyChanged published` log line with its `source` and a per-process running `count`, so the rate per call site can be read from production logs. The stream still yields once on every subscribe. App bundles from before 2026-10-12 stamp their offline catalogue viewer marker from that first event. Current bundles stamp it when the signed-in session finds no marker (below), so the first yield can go once the older bundles have been replaced (#6406).

The app refreshes its own screens after its own privacy writes: the account switch, an audience, a follower, a follow request or an access grant. Queries on screen refetch without blanking and the rest refetch when next shown; its identity, credentials, unsynced ticks, offline download bookkeeping, developer tools and static reference data are left alone. Its own write never narrows what this viewer can read, so nothing is withdrawn. A change someone else makes reaches the app on its next read. The website withdraws every cached client query except its auth-token and privacy-settings transport queries and drops the persisted cache (which only ever holds the viewer's own profile queries). It re-renders the server markup only on pages that server-render other people's protected content (walls, sessions, profiles, playlists, setters, gyms and climb pages); other pages keep theirs. It listens on the tab's one shared realtime socket, the same one live comment threads use.

Downloaded catalogues are gated by a persistent viewer marker that binds catalogue access to a SHA-256 digest of the credential last checked against the authenticated profile API; tokens are never stored in SQLite. The same credential can use downloads offline after restart. A missing marker, another account, or a changed credential requires online revalidation. Native and Expo web revalidate in two cases only: when the signed-in session finds no marker at all (a new install, a catalogue wiped at sign-out, a bundle that never stamped one, or a withdrawal that never finished), checked when catalogue authorization starts for the account and when a database handle is published; and when a local read finds a marker for an older credential (another account, or the token refresh that comes about once a week). Revalidation clears cached protected catalogue rows and spray media while retaining the viewer's own ticks, outbox and drafts, resets pull checkpoints so allowed rows are fetched again, and stamps the marker for the current credential. Local reads return their unavailable fallback during withdrawal; a failed purge fails closed. Online authenticated reads continue immediately through the server, without waiting for SQLite or enriching/rescuing from withdrawn local copies. In-process invalidation epochs discard older reads even after a replacement catalogue is ready. The current viewer's rows and every queued climb reference are preserved. Another account's draft without a positive server sync sequence is retained as possible unsynced work, and a write still waiting to send keeps the climb it names. A dead letter keeps nothing: it sends again only if revived, its replay carries its own payload, and it can sit in the bin indefinitely. While kept rows remain, local catalogue reads stay blocked and server reads continue. The pass then writes a held record (`privacy:catalog-held:v1`: viewer and credential digest) instead of the marker. Each later launch, and each delivered or dead-lettered write, counts only the kept rows and stamps the marker once none are left; it withdraws nothing, aborts no download and asks the server nothing. Downloaded foreign drafts with a positive server sequence are replaceable cache, since `is_draft` is mirrored from the server and does not identify local work. In-progress editor drafts live in a separate preference store and are untouched. A pass that removes rows resets every checkpoint and completion marker and clears the holds index, so every enabled board replays; a pass that removes nothing keeps all three. Catalogue authorization serializes repairs, retains one trailing repair for requests received during a run, and retries a failure after 1, 5 and 15 seconds while foregrounded and online. Schema/handle availability, reconnect and foreground resume a retained failure; account or handle replacement retires obsolete attempts. Successful repair wakes enabled downloads for authorized replay. The viewer's own privacy writes never revalidate the catalogue. When another account changes a climb this phone has already downloaded, a switch to draft or unlisted arrives with the next sync and local lists stop showing it. A narrower audience that hides the climb from this viewer is different: the server stops sending the row, so the downloaded copy stays until the board is downloaded again or the next revalidation. Previously delivered files cannot be recalled from someone else's storage.

Native catalogue revalidation handles SQLite contention with the existing background write policy: at most three attempts with 250 ms gaps and a 20-second retry budget for each write task. Withdrawal of the persistent viewer marker completes before the authenticated profile request, including when revalidation is requested offline or backgrounded; authorization then waits for a foreground connection. An automatic token refresh during authorization triggers one additional profile request; only a digest stable across that authenticated response can authorize the catalogue. Credential checks also surround marker stamping and completion. A current read that finds an existing marker for an older token requests recovery; startup probes once catalogue authorization starts for the account, so an earlier mismatch cannot be lost. Each catalogue transaction takes the immediate write lock before reading and retries on a fresh connection. Exhausted retries, non-lock errors and changed credentials keep catalogue reads blocked; personal ticks, drafts and pending mutations retain the same protection.

Solo queue persistence stores climb references, order and mirror choices, without copied climb details or attribution. Legacy snapshots are sanitized when read. A credential change (sign-in or sign-out) retires Undo offers and older asynchronous queue work; current queue entries lose copied details until an authorized read restores them. The auxiliary playlist swipe track is cleared on withdrawal and is not persisted; reopening the playlist restores that track after checking its permissions. Drafts and the mutation outbox remain intact.

Playlist editing also withdraws copied climb details and retires outstanding edit callbacks. The active-board file stores only the selected wall reference, layout, local angle and device configuration. Names, owner details and location require a current authorized response; offline startup uses a generic board label.

Kiosk previews, watch polling, session discovery and Live Activity pushes reauthorize current climb details. Quiet privacy changes withdraw copied kiosk and lock-screen content. APNs recipients are checked against current grants, and updates prepared before invalidation cannot follow the clearing push on the same server. Revoked sessions receive an empty, immediately dismissed Live Activity. The watch endpoint changes its sequence when redacting a climb and returns the existing session-ended response when session access is revoked, so installed watches clear their prior display.

The board sync behind an offline download sends a draft only to the account in its `user_id`; a draft with no Boardsesh owner, such as one imported from a Kilter or Tension account, reaches no device. Unlisted and published climbs sync to every device, whoever set them: the author's account privacy is not consulted. Before #6355 the sync left a private account's climbs out of other climbers' pulls; migration 0276 re-stamps them, with their stats and grades, so a phone that pulled before receives them on its next pull. A phone that downloaded someone else's draft before this rule keeps it until that board is downloaded again: there is no withdrawal marker (owner decision, 2026-10-13). A draft another account owns also goes at the phone's next catalogue revalidation, which purges other accounts' rows; an ownerless draft looks like manufacturer data to that pass and stays.

Static snapshots now contain external, unowned, published catalog data only (no drafts, nor their stats, grades or beta) and carry `privacyVersion: 1` in manifest/artifact format 2. Shipped format-1 clients reject this changed dataset and use authenticated paged sync, so their old watermark logic cannot skip authored climbs. Authored content comes from the viewer-authorized API. Old public artifacts must be removed from managed origins and CDN caches before enabling controls. See [snapshot rollout](board-snapshots.md#account-privacy-and-snapshot-rollout) and [dataset contract](board-snapshots-dataset.md).

Privacy controls cover Boardsesh activity and account associations. Imported manufacturer setter names and first-ascent credits remain part of the external public catalog; making a Boardsesh account private does not rewrite those external credits. Independently imported manufacturer beta links follow the same rule. Both Boardsesh beta creation paths stamp the authenticated creator; upstream sync preserves that ownership on conflict, so a later import cannot turn a Boardsesh upload into an unowned public link. Online reads (`setterProfile`'s `linkedUser*`, the setter hero) do not name the private Boardsesh account behind an external setter name, though a published climb's `user_id` in the board sync can connect the two. First-ascent credits produced from Boardsesh ticks are a tick rule, not a climb rule: the credit is the earliest send the current viewer may read under the tick, account and resource policies, so a hidden first send passes the credit to the next visible one rather than leaving it blank. The date is that same send's (`privateSafeFirstAscentAt`), never the stored `fa_at`, which dates the earliest send of any visibility. The www climb page applies the same projection for an anonymous visitor, so it never shows a private sender or a deleted author's stale stored name.

## Release sequence

1. Apply the generated migration and deploy backend/web read enforcement with `BOARDSESH_PRIVACY_ENABLED` unset. Read enforcement is always active; the flag gates settings and publication controls.
2. Regenerate format-2 privacy snapshots, replace manifests and withdraw older public artifacts and cached previews. Validate anonymous, owner, accepted follower, pending follower and removed follower reads against the primary database.
3. Ship native and Expo web clients with audience controls, cache withdrawal and the Settings → Privacy page. Verify stale client mutations preserve restrictions and stale public drafts are rejected.
4. Enable `BOARDSESH_PRIVACY_ENABLED=1` on the backend and web account issuer after backend enforcement and artifact cleanup are confirmed. Nobody is sent through a setup screen: existing accounts keep their setting and change it in Settings. The deprecated `privacyOnboardingVersion` field always reads 1, so app builds that still carry the retired setup screen never open it, and its input is accepted and ignored.
5. Watch denied reads, mutation errors, invalidation delivery and catalog replay cost. Roll back controls by disabling the flag while retaining enforcement and additive database tables; never roll back to a reader that ignores privacy.

Settings → Privacy holds the analytics and session recording switches, the Public or Private choice, session defaults, "stay anonymous on boards I don't own", a home-board review, follow requests and followers, and the privacy policy. Exporting your climbing data lives on Settings → Account. Settings, approvals and audience labels are translated in English, Spanish, French and German. The desktop settings page hands off to the same Expo app settings, while surviving web profiles use the same follow approvals and private activity boundary.

## Regression coverage

The database privacy matrix exercises self, accepted and pending followers, strangers, Only me, explicit Public consent and stale revisions, parent resources, comment ancestors (including deleted ones), reply authors, the owner of each item type, private and public gym threads for every role `gym` checks, deleted-comment events on a live thread, private moderators and voters in a proposer's inbox, beta attachments, notifications and follower removal while checking aggregate retention. Separate session tests cover merges/splits, board history projection and invalidation during quiet subscriptions. Client tests cover publication revisions, catalogue authorization at credential boundaries and snapshot compatibility; public preview and sitemap tests check current authorization rather than cached visibility.

Run the backend suites against their PostgreSQL/Redis test services. Do not run two independent backend test processes against the same database: each process resets its schema. The manual tester flows are in the worktree's `.boardsesh/qa-notes.md` and the PR test plan.

Social test tables must use the production `social_entity_type` enum. Using text instead hides PostgreSQL comparison and recursive UNION errors. The shared privacy helpers normalize incoming entity types to text because `content_privacy` also covers non-social types such as playlists and beta links. Real resolver tests cover unread notification counts, materialized activity feeds and bulk session votes with these enum columns.

The privacy matrix also covers empty inboxes, exact unread counts, read and other-recipient exclusions, aliased flat and grouped inboxes, private comment ancestors and retained ownerless policies.

Spray-wall presence uses the same held UUID in the current wall-policy gate, every stream recheck and climb-content redaction. Published climb details remain visible only on that exact spray layout; draft, item-audience and author-identity rules still apply separately. Cached hardest-send names also pass current catalog/item/layout checks; missing catalog rows retain aggregate grades and the Unknown fallback without a copied name. Queue producers do not publish capability-derived details: a permitted subscriber rebuilds its own current snapshot after each wake-up. Unlisted UUIDs remain valid only while the wall is unhidden, active and readable under its effective policy; numeric IDs and layout IDs never supply that capability. Admin hiding also prevents new membership stamps and rejects reports from previously stamped non-owner members before their side effects. The wall's leaderboard, keyed on its uuid, opens for the same holder while the wall is unhidden. First-party presence clients forward only the held UUID paired with the resolved numeric board, and clear their request/cache scope when that capability changes. A server denial (a `NOT_FOUND` read, or a live stream the server ended on its access recheck) retires the reader and clears the loaded wall, its pages and bylines; a transport failure, including the socket giving up reconnecting, only turns the live flag off and keeps the wall and refresh.

Serial lookup never supplies a hidden board link. Authenticated serial lookups use current no-link policy; owners and approved readers keep their board UUID. The same reading covers every serial path: the connect resolver's candidate list (a hidden candidate carries an empty uuid, its board type as its name and no location), the remembered serial pointer `myBoardSerialConfigs` returns (no uuid or slug for a board the caller cannot read without its link, though the connect still routes there), and the `BOARD_SERIAL_EXISTS` error, which names the existing board only to a caller who may read it without its link. The app binds a serial pick by its numeric id and pairs a uuid only when the climber already held it and it verifies as the chosen board. Anonymous hidden-board results contain geometry only, with an empty UUID/slug, null presence ID, generic name and no owner or UGC. Existing clients may need to normalize that empty identity to absent and key generic scan rows by serial/config; #6392 tracks the affected follow-heal and quickstart paths. A cached selection naming a merged loser UUID may require opening a fresh board or share selection to receive the authorized canonical UUID. Fresh server selections and share-link redemption already return that canonical identity; a numeric resolution never manufactures it. This older cached-selection normalization remains in #6392.

Session path changes attribute link presentation to the participant who made that change. Creation does not prove who presented a later mutable path. Roster snapshots, resubscriptions and session payload reads use ordinary board standing; a caller who explicitly supplied the path keeps it. Link-only readers can receive geometry on reconnect until they present or identify their board again. Gym edit authority never opens a board's presence. A gym owner, admin or editor, or a community admin or leader whose role covers the gym, reads a linked board's presence by that board's own policy, the standing `gymBoards` lists boards to them by: Public boards, and boards they own, hold a grant on or inherit as a follower. An Unlisted board also opens when they present its uuid, and the kiosk preview forwards each slot's uuid, so an Unlisted slot board shows its presence there; a stranger's Invite-only or Only-me board linked to the gym stays not-found, as `board(uuid)` is.
